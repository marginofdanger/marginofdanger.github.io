Evidence

Claims by section
business-model
  • Marketers, most of them small businesses buying self-serve, bid in a real-time auction for each impression; revenue is ad impressions times average price per ad.
  • There are no multi-year contracts or take-or-pay terms; deferred revenue was $1,080M at December 2025, 0.5% of revenue, and receivables ran about 36 days (10-K FY2025, Note 1 and Note 2).
  • Compounding the disclosed growth rates for 2020 through 2025 (from the 2019 level) gives impressions up 2.77x and price per ad up 2%, a product of 2.83x against actual ad revenue growth of 2.82x ($69,655M to $196,175M). Almost all six-year growth was volume.
  • Over 2022-2025, in log terms, DAP growth supplied about 35% of ad revenue growth, more impressions per person about 50% and price per ad about 16%.
  • Asia-Pacific grows by volume at flat prices, and US & Canada grows by price.
  • The range is 7-11% of revenue, about $15-22bn on FY2025 [estimate: billing-versus-user gap as the minimum; press figure as the maximum].
  • The January 2025 extension of server lives to 5.5 years cut FY2025 depreciation by $2.92bn, worth up to 1.5 points of FoA margin (10-K FY2025, Note 1).
  • Each incremental FoA revenue dollar produced $0.83 of operating income in 2024, $0.42 in 2025 and $0.13 in H1 2026.
  • FoA employee compensation rose 28% in 2025 to $39,943M and 45% in H1 2026 to $26,650M while company headcount declined 1% y/y to 75,472
  • The 2026 expense guide of $165-169bn, less RL costs of $21.5-22.5bn, puts FoA costs at $142.5-147.5bn, +48% to +53%, and with consensus revenue gives a 2026 FoA incremental margin of 3-13% [estimate: guide less RL; BBG FY2026 revenue $254.1bn less ~$2.3bn RL].
  • A dilution that shows in its own gross margin means EssilorLuxottica books the retail or wholesale sale of most units, and Meta's RL revenue includes only Meta's share plus Meta.com sales.
  • Against a blended 2025 retail price of about $330-400, Meta books roughly 25-50% of retail value per unit.
  • RL revenue has been flat for five years: $2,274M (2021), $2,159M, $1,896M, $2,146M, $2,207M.
  • The other 87-93% of RL cost is program spend set by budget. At a 20-30% contribution margin, break-even on about $19-20bn of program cost needs $65-100bn of RL revenue, 30-45x FY2025 [estimate: fixed cost ÷ contribution margin].
  • Against 2025 revenue estimates, VR loses about $5-5.5bn a year and wearables about $14bn [estimate].
capital-allocation
  • Capex was to "come down as a percent of revenue over the long term" (Li, Q3 2022 call), and by Q4 2024 it was "really too early to determine what long-run capital intensity is going to look like."
competitive-advantage
  • The ad business has an advantage that has survived well-funded attacks and earns returns far above its cost of capital, and that now requires heavy reinvestment to defend.
  • FoA operating margin was 51.6% in FY2025 against 40.7% for Google Services, and FoA after-tax return on invested capital was about 43-46% [estimate: FoA operating income taxed at 21% over average consolidated invested capital, less a 0-5% RL share] against a cost of capital of about 10%.
  • Consolidated ROIC declined from 44.0% (2021) to 35.2% (FY2025) and 28.6% on a trailing basis to June 2026, and the return on capital added in 2025 alone was about 17%.
  • In Q2 2026 FoA margin was 38.8%, below Google Services' 41.8% for the first time since at least 2020; before that quarter's charges it was about 43-45%.
  • The advantage used to come from a friend graph with near-zero marginal cost, and that source is shrinking.
  • What still separates Meta is the combination of 3.6bn daily users, owned inventory that pays no traffic-acquisition cost, and conversion data from millions of self-serve advertisers.
  • FoA's 11-14 point margin premium over Google Services in 2020-2025 (excluding 2022) is consistent with that cost.
  • minimum efficient scale in 2026 is $130-145bn of capex plus $349.31bn of commitments, which keeps Snap, Pinterest and Reddit from matching Meta's conversion performance and does not stop Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon or ByteDance.
  • Against Alphabet, the peer closest in scale and model, META's consolidated margin premium narrowed by 6 points from 2020 to 2025, and its ROIC premium from 8.0 to 1.9 points as META's invested capital grew 2.9x against Alphabet's 3.1x.
  • Reality Labs has no durable competitive advantage in the financial sense.
  • RL revenue grew about 14% a year from 2020 to 2025 while RL costs grew about 23% a year; since 2022 the rates are 0.7% and 10.5%.
  • That position is based on the exclusive design, manufacturing and optical-retail partnership with EssilorLuxottica and on a lead in on-device AI, displays and EMG input funded by nearly $100bn of cumulative spend.
competitive-landscape
  • Its ad revenue was 57.3% of Google advertising's in FY2020, 66.6% in FY2025 and 72.7% in Q2 2026.
  • Meta's share of the pool rose 1.3 points in five years while Alphabet's declined 5.8 points and Amazon's rose 4.0.
  • Of the FY2024-to-FY2025 increase for the three scale sellers ($78.1bn), Meta accounted for $35.5bn (45.5%), Google $30.1bn and Amazon $12.4bn; in Q2 2026 Meta accounted for $12.8bn of $27.2bn (47.3%).
  • Meta's share is about 78-79% excluding YouTube and 67-68% including it; the HHI is about 6,400 and 5,000 on the two definitions [estimate].
  • Customer power is low; supplier power is held by Apple and Google, which take no share of Meta's ad dollar but set the signal rules on the devices where "substantially all" DAP access FoA (10-K FY2025).
  • TikTok's ex-China ad revenue grew about 40% in 2025 to about $33bn [web estimate], about 17% of Meta's ad revenue.
  • RL competes in two markets moving in opposite directions: AI glasses, where Meta leads by volume and the category grew 2-4x a year, and VR headsets, where Meta leads a market that is shrinking and its share is declining.
  • Omdia counted 8.7m AI-glasses units in 2025, of which Meta shipped about 7.4m (85.2%)
  • In display AI glasses, Meta Ray-Ban Display shipped 84,000 of about 730,000 units in 2025 (about 12%), and Chinese vendors (Rokid, Alibaba, Even Realities) held 71% (Omdia [web]).
  • Meta's 2025 RL cost base of $21.4bn was 4.2x IDC's 2026 smart-glasses revenue forecast of $5.1bn and about 12x the global VR/MR hardware pool [estimate].
expectations
  • Management's expense guide is conservative when Meta is cutting and close to accurate when it is building, and its initial capex guide has become a minimum.
history
  • Meta issues blunt multi-year warnings (mid-30s margins in 2018, the ~$10bn ATT sizing in 2022, "notably larger" capex growth in 2025) that set off one-day declines of 11-26%.
m-and-a
  • Instagram (2012) and WhatsApp (2014), Meta's two successful large acquisitions, account for $17.7bn of accounting consideration and 64% of the $24.5bn goodwill balance at December 2025, and both have billions of daily users.
management-claims
  • With costs flat, cutting the loss by $2bn needs RL revenue to rise from $2.2bn to at least $4.2bn before hardware costs on the added revenue.
  • With it and consensus revenue of $254.1bn, 2026 operating income is $75-79bn, 5-10% below 2025's $83,276M [estimate].
secular-trends
  • From 2021 to 2025 Meta's advertising revenue grew with the digital ad market, and all of it came from volume.
  • The average price therefore mixes a true per-impression price with surface and regional mix, and the filings do not separate them.
  • Meta lost about 3 points of US share in 2022 and took four years to recover it; from 2021 to 2025 its US&C ad revenue compounded at 12.4% against 11.7% for the US market.
  • Meta grew with the closed-loop performance cohort (Amazon, AppLovin, Reddit) from 2023 onward.
  • Q2 2026 constant-currency ad growth of 26% against US internet growth of 13.9% in 2025 and WPP's +8.9% is a gap of about 12-17 points that events, currency and macro cannot account for, so most of it is share gain.
  • That is about 2.5 points a year against about 0.75 points a year from 2020 to 2025. The path needs a rising share of digital, material non-ad revenue (under 2% today), or a market growing much faster than agency forecasts.
  • DAP of 3.58bn is about 73% of internet users outside China [estimate: 3.58 ÷ (6.0 − 1.1bn); ITU and CNNIC-derived figures [web]], and DAP grew about as fast as the internet population in the year to June 2026.
technology
  • Meta's technical advantage is concentrated in the ranking and recommendation systems that choose which post and which ad each of 3.6bn daily users sees.
  • Each reported lift is small: 3-6% conversion gains on specific surfaces in 2025, and 8.3% more ad clicks and 15.7% more conversions on Facebook from user-understanding models in Q2 2026 (earnings calls).
  • The step between Q4 2024 and Q3 2025 coincided with a scope change from shopping-only to all end-to-end objectives and with automation becoming the default in campaign setup, so run-rate growth mostly measures product consolidation.
  • Muse Spark (April 2026), the first MSL model and Meta's first frontier model withheld as closed weights, scored 52 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index behind Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6, and 1427 on the agentic GDPval-AA benchmark against 1676 for GPT-5.4
  • The infrastructure is a cost and supply-access advantage; hyperscalers and neoclouds sell comparable capacity, and Meta buys from them.
value-chain
  • Apple and Google are outside the chain, taking nothing from the ad dollar and controlling the signals that set targeting quality.
  • In 2026 accelerator vendors earn gross profit of about 13-20 cents per Meta revenue dollar on Meta's purchases [estimate: 18-26c × 70-75% vendor gross margin], equal to roughly 40-60% of Meta's own operating profit
  • Compute capacity and its funding are the binding constraint today, and the scarcest input, judged by who is earning margin, is memory, then foundry and packaging.
  • At the median, the typical Meta advertiser loses money on the first order and depends on repeat purchases, so Meta's price depends on advertisers' lifetime-value assumptions and on attribution quality, which Apple controls on iOS.
  • Price per ad rose about 14% cumulatively from the 2020 level to 2025 (about 2.6% a year) while capex rose 4.6x, so revenue has grown through volume and conversion without passing input-cost inflation through as price.
  • Operating income rose $36.5bn from 2023 to 2025 on cumulative 2023-2025 capex of $139.5bn, a pre-tax return of about 26% [estimate: ignores RL, timing lags and the 2025 useful-life extension].
